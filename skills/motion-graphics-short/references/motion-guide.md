# Motion-graphics guide

## Design before choreography

Define the frame's static hierarchy first. If a key state is confusing while paused, animation will not fix it. Use a constrained system: usually one primary typeface family, one display treatment, a small palette, and repeated spatial rules.

## Give motion a job

- **Reveal:** disclose information in the order it should be understood.
- **Transform:** show a relationship by changing one object into another.
- **Track:** move a marker/camera so the viewer follows a process.
- **Compare:** align states so differences become visible.
- **Punctuate:** use a brief scale, impact, or color change on the crucial beat.
- **Connect:** carry direction, shape, color, or velocity across a scene boundary.

Choose one main motion idea per beat. Avoid perpetual floating/wobble that makes the composition feel alive but communicates nothing.

## Timing

Use faster entrances for already-familiar labels and longer holds for claims, diagrams, or surprising numbers. Match exit direction to the next entrance where it creates continuity. Do not rely on the same pop-in easing for every element.

## Data and claims

Label axes/units or provide enough context for a number to mean what the narration claims. Attribute external statistics in delivery notes or on screen when appropriate. A count-up should stop at a verified value, not a decorative approximation.

## Render reliability

Keep timelines deterministic and seekable. Freeze remote resources locally, wait for fonts/media, and verify first/middle/last frames of every scene. Inspect seam backgrounds for flashes and confirm motion blur/filters survive encoding.
