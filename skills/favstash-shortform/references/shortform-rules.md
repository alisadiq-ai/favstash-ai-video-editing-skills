# Short-form editing rules

These are operating ranges, not universal laws. The creator's intent, source material, accessibility needs, and platform preview win when they conflict with a default.

## Canvas and delivery

- Default video canvas: 1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps.
- Default delivery: H.264, `yuv420p`, AAC, fast-start MP4.
- Optimize the included styles for roughly 5–60 seconds. Support up to 90 seconds when the story earns it; route longer work elsewhere.
- Default carousel canvas: 1080×1350 (4:5). Switch to 1080×1080 only for a destination or brief that needs square.
- Preview at phone size and inspect the actual encoded output, not only the source composition.

## Platform-safe composition

For a generic 1080×1920 export, begin with a conservative critical-content rectangle around `x=90..900` and `y=220..1550`. The right-side interaction rail and bottom caption/navigation areas vary by platform, account, device, and posting mode, so this is deliberately conservative—not an official permanent spec.

- Keep hooks, faces, product controls, and essential captions inside the critical rectangle unless a destination-specific preview proves a better placement.
- Decorative backgrounds and full-bleed B-roll may extend to every edge.
- Do not put critical text behind the creator's own burned-in subtitles.
- Re-check platform UI when a publishing destination is known because overlays change.

## Hooks and narrative

- Make the first comprehensible beat immediately relevant. It can be a promise, tension, surprising evidence, or a vivid moment; it must be honest.
- A useful miniature structure is `hook → context/proof → turn/payoff → optional action`, but omit stages the idea does not need.
- One short should make one primary point. Split competing ideas into variants.
- Change the information or visual state before attention goes stale. Do not cut merely because a timer elapsed.
- Let emotionally meaningful pauses breathe. Compress dead air, repetition, and setup the viewer cannot use.

## Typography

Start with these 1080-wide ranges and adjust after phone preview:

- main hook: roughly 72–120 px;
- supporting statement: roughly 54–84 px;
- captions: roughly 46–68 px;
- labels or evidence: roughly 34–52 px when truly secondary.

Prefer one or two lines, short line lengths, strong contrast, and a visible hierarchy. Use a font weight that survives video compression. Add a restrained shadow, panel, or stroke only when the background needs separation. Avoid long all-caps passages. Verify names, numbers, and line breaks manually.

## Motion and transitions

- Motion should direct the eye, reveal hierarchy, explain a relationship, or create a deliberate emotional beat.
- Hard cuts and invisible continuity are the default. Use wipes, zooms, spins, glitches, and whips only when motivated by content or brand.
- Give entrances and exits enough time to read; avoid stacking several competing animations.
- If everything moves, nothing feels important.

## Audio

- Speech intelligibility comes first. Duck music under voice and inspect on phone speakers and headphones.
- Use short fades around cuts to avoid clicks.
- Choose SFX as punctuation—commonly one to three noticeable cues across a 12-second calm edit, more in a deliberately energetic style, and zero when silence is stronger.
- Never infer reuse rights from the ability to download a song or social-platform audio.

## Editorial integrity

- Do not promise virality, fabricate social proof, or create a misleading before/after.
- Preserve the speaker's intended meaning when trimming.
- Transform references into original work; identify what performs as a hypothesis to test.
- Carry rights, attribution, and disclosure requirements into the asset ledger and delivery notes.
