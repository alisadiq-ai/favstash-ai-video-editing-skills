# Short-form mixing guide

## Priority

1. spoken meaning;
2. intentional native sound/foley;
3. music's emotional support;
4. transition and emphasis effects.

When layers compete, reduce or remove a lower-priority layer before trying more processing.

## Voice

Remove obvious hum/noise only as far as the voice remains natural. Match gain and room tone across cuts. Use gentle high-pass/EQ/compression when needed; do not chase one universal preset. Inspect peaks and listen for sibilance, pumping, clipped syllables, and breaths cut unnaturally.

## Music

Choose music whose tempo, density, and emotional claim fit the piece. Use only owned, licensed, public-domain, or appropriately Creative-Commons audio. Duck under speech and automate larger changes at narrative turns. A brief music drop can create more impact than another SFX.

## Bundled SFX roles

- clicks/tick: interface action or tiny text event;
- clean pop/ding/sparkle: reveal or positive payoff;
- whooshes: visible motivated travel or scene handoff;
- riser: anticipation that resolves;
- sub-impact: one major claim or reveal;
- error buzz: unmistakable error/contrast, usually playful.

Start subtle. A calm 12-second reel may need zero to three noticeable cues; an energetic demo may need more. The final decision comes from listening, not counting.

## Delivery checks

- no digital clipping or unexplained loudness jumps;
- speech understandable on a phone at ordinary volume;
- fades prevent clicks and the ending is intentional;
- stereo content does not disappear or become harsh in mono playback;
- ledger and attribution cover all music and non-original recordings.
