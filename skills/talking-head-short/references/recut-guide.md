# Talking-head recut guide

## Find the spine

Mark the best standalone statement, the minimum context it needs, the evidence/example, and the resolution. A later sentence may be the strongest opening, but moving it must not change chronology or imply a conclusion the speaker did not make.

## Trim with ears and eyes

- Cut repeated thoughts, throat-clearing, and delays that add no personality.
- Preserve micro-pauses around emotion, humor, difficult claims, and transitions.
- Add tiny audio crossfades at speech edits; listen for chopped consonants and room-tone jumps.
- When a visual jump remains, decide whether it feels intentionally energetic. Cover it only with relevant B-roll, evidence, a motivated crop, or a purposeful graphic.

## Framing

Keep eyes comfortably above center while reserving room for captions. Track the face when the source is horizontal; avoid frantic automated reframing. Use punch-ins to emphasize a real turn, hide a necessary edit, or vary a long stable shot—not on every sentence.

## Overlays

Prefer evidence to decoration: the actual product view, number, quote source, diagram, or object being discussed. Keep lower thirds brief. Do not let captions, headline text, and a proof card compete simultaneously.

## Integrity review

Watch the edited speech without graphics. Confirm the speaker would recognize the meaning, qualifiers remain attached to their claims, and no reaction shot is falsely paired with different context.
