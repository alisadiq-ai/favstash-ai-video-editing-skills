# Caption guide

## Models

- **Quiet rail:** stable two-line region, lightly animated phrase changes. Best default for dense talking-head or educational speech.
- **Phrase cards:** larger, shorter statements that can move within safe composition space. Useful for narration and text-led work.
- **Selective embed:** a small number of emphasized words or phrases integrated into the scene. Use alongside or instead of a quiet rail only when it has a clear hierarchy.

Do not reserve a permanent empty bottom band merely because captions exist. Treat captions as an overlay in the full composition while protecting their local contrast and avoiding other critical elements.

## Segmentation

Break on meaning, breath, and syntax. Keep closely related words together. Avoid leaving articles/prepositions alone or showing the next phrase too early. A phrase may span one or two lines; rewrite line breaks without rewriting the spoken words.

Use reading speed as a warning signal, not an automatic deletion rule. When a phrase is too dense, hold it longer, choose a quieter visual, or use a clearly labeled summary treatment.

## Styling

Use the creator's licensed font and high contrast. Start around 46–68 px on a 1080-wide canvas and verify on a phone. Use a restrained background panel, shadow, or stroke only as needed. One accent color and weight change are usually enough.

## Accuracy checklist

- names, handles, brands, acronyms, dates, prices, percentages, and units;
- negations and qualifiers such as “not,” “may,” and “up to”;
- speaker changes and off-screen dialogue;
- translated meaning and disclosure of translation when material;
- no caption hidden behind app UI or another text layer;
- no caption extending beyond the spoken audio because of stale timing.
